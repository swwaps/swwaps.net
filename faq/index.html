What is a dragon boat?

A long, narrow canoe seating 20 paddlers in 10 rows, with a drummer at the bow near the dragon's head and a steersperson at the stern. The dragon head and tail usually come out for competitions, not every practice.

What is dragon boating?

Modern dragon boat racing took off in Hong Kong in 1976, but its roots go back more than 2,000 years in China. During races, crews paddle in sync down a 250m or 500m course to the finish line.

How is it different from rowing?

Rowing comes from Western tradition; dragon boating from Eastern. Rowers sit single file facing backward and row with oars. Dragon boaters sit two abreast in 10 rows, face forward, and use paddles.

Do I need experience?

None at all — that's the best part. We welcome paddlers aged 14 and up, at every fitness level and background, with nothing more than curiosity.

What happens at a practice?

On-water practices last 60–90 minutes: technique drills and race pieces, with interval, endurance, and power training woven in. All of it in a dragon boat.

What should I bring to my first practice?

A water bottle, sunglasses or a brimmed hat if it's sunny, and waterproof sunscreen. Wear comfortable clothes and shoes you don't mind getting wet — quick-drying synthetics are best.

When are practices? Do I have to attend all of them?

Sundays 8:30–10 am and Wednesdays 7:30–9 pm, weather permitting. With membership you can attend as many as you like — but if you want to race, race-team seats depend largely on practice attendance.

How often do you race, and where?

Four races a season, with a separate fee for teammates who race. For 2026: two Portland races, plus Ridgefield WA and Olympia WA.

What does the team do off the water?

We're actually friends outside of training — movie nights, barbecues, team hikes, birthday celebrations, and more.

How do I try it or sign up for a practice?

Easy — contact us and we'll get you on the boat. There's no charge for your first three practices.
